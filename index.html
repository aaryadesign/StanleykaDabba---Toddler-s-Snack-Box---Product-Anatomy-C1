Onboarding characters

Built from the character parts already in the app (body, eyes, cheeks, mouth). No new artwork.

Entrance
The character rises in with a little squash, then opens its eyes. The text follows a beat later.
Breathing
A slow swell every few seconds, so the screen is never quite still.
Blinking
At random moments, sometimes twice.
Looking
Pupils follow your cursor or finger. While you type, it watches the text appear. When nothing happens, it glances around.
Expressions
The face changes with what you do. A curious “o” when a field is empty, a smile and blush once there’s a name, a grin with happy ^ ^ eyes on Next. A wrong PIN gets a frown and droopy lids until you fix it. The meal character licks its lips at the food. Leave it alone and it dozes off; move and it wakes with a start.
Reactions
Tap it: squish, surprise, then a giggle. Typing makes it chatter. A full PIN gets a happy hop, a wrong one a head shake. Monthly gets a bigger grin than weekly.

Try: type a name, then press Next. On Location, press Next with a short PIN. Or stop moving for 15 seconds.

    Tomorrow’s box

    Your “Box for tomorrow” screen as it is in the app. The only change is the food photo, which is now the live 3D box packed with this menu.

    Unboxing
    It drops in and bounces. Closed, it shakes every few seconds like something inside wants out, with a “Tap to open!” bubble. Nothing inside shows until it’s opened.
    Comes to life
    On tap it unlatches, the lid flies up with confetti, the camera leans in, and each compartment pops full one after another.
    What’s inside
    Count tags sit right on the food. Tap one (or the food) to glide in: a card shows how much, and which of the menu’s ingredients it’s made from, using the app’s own ingredient icons. ‹ › steps through them.
    Ingredients
    Tap an ingredient further down the screen to jump up and see where it is in the box. The list looks the same as before.
    Zoom to read
    Pinch, or scroll once you’ve touched the box. It stays where you leave it; if it leaves the frame it glides back.
